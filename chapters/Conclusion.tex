This thesis successfully explored the scalability and throughput bottlenecks of distributed logs by aiming to mitigate the performance degradation of fast-path consensus during collision-heavy scenarios

The conceptualization and design of the "Conformal Consensus" architecture established a new mechanism for managing optimistic fast-path trade-offs.

The latency reductions observed during testing were highly dependent on a specifically engineered, bursty, and low-contention workload designed to create artificial "windows of opportunity" for the fast path.

Ultimately, while the viability of Conformal Consensus in production environments is at least questionable, this research provides a mathematically sound exploratory framework that further explores the application of conformal methods to distributed data management.
